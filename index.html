What is your vote worth?

Every Lok Sabha seat sends one MP, whatever the number of voters behind them. Pick your constituency to see how many people share your MP and what that does to the weight of one vote.

    How the number is worked out

    Vote value = average electors per seat in India divided by electors in your seat. A value of 1.00 means your vote carries the average weight. Above 1 means fewer voters share your MP, so each vote counts for more. Below 1 means more voters share your MP.

    Share of your MP is 1 divided by the electors in your seat. Rank 1 is the seat where one vote counts most.

    Data: electors per parliamentary constituency for the 2024 general election, compiled from the Election Commission of India phase-wise annexures published through PIB. These counts leave out service electors, so seats can be slightly higher in the official totals. Surat is approximate (about 17.67 lakh, from news reports) and Outer Manipur is the sum of its two polling phases. Seat boundaries and the 543 total are unchanged since 2008, so these figures describe the House as it stands in 2026.

    This measures how many voters share one MP. It does not measure how well an MP performs, or how many people actually turned out.
